Between every phase not libuv's

            

              
    
                
  • process.nextTick queue — drained first. Owned by Node, not libuv and not V8.
    • 
                
  • Microtask queue — drained next. The queue itself lives in V8; Node just decides when to tell V8 to empty it.
    • 
              

              

                Neither is a phase. They cut in front of the whole cycle, which is why a promise always beats a timer,
                and why an infinite nextTick chain can starve the loop libuv is trying to run.

            

          

          

            
Why it matters

            

              
    
                
  • There is no single "task queue" in Node. setTimeout and setImmediate live in different phases — you cannot reason about them as one FIFO.
    • 
                
  • At the top level, setTimeout(fn,0) vs setImmediate is nondeterministic — it depends on how long startup took before the loop reached timers.
    • 
                
  • Inside an I/O callback you are in poll, so check is next: setImmediate always wins. Guaranteed.
    • 
                
  • Work finished by a pool thread also lands in poll — the worker cannot run your JS, it can only queue it.
    • 
                
  • The loop advances between your callbacks, never during them. A busy stack freezes it mid-cycle.
    •
